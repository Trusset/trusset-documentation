---
title: "Investor Profiles"
description: "Your compliance policy as configuration, applied on every onboarding path and enforced on-chain"
---

An investor profile turns your onboarding policy into configuration. It decides who may onboard, which documents they provide, which countries you refuse, and how each customer is classified. Every onboarding path of your instance applies the same profile. The result is recorded on-chain, where a token, a lending market or a vault checks it before anything moves.

## What a profile controls

The identity check runs through Sumsub, on the level you name for each profile. On your instance ID register the investor type is written on-chain as the investor class of the wallet's attestation. An ERC-3643 identity registry carries no investor type, so there it stays on the customer record.

## Start from a preset

Every instance starts with three presets:

| Profile | Classifies as | Admits | Documents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retail | Retail | Natural persons | Government ID, selfie and liveness |
| Professional | Professional | Natural persons, professional clients only | Government ID, selfie and liveness, proof of address |
| Eligible Counterparty | Eligible counterparty | Legal entities, professional clients only | Government ID, selfie and liveness, proof of address, company documents |

The presets cannot be deleted, and their name, classification and professional-client rule are fixed. Their documents, level and blocked countries can be adjusted. When your policy differs, add a custom profile, for example a professional profile for one jurisdiction with its own blocked countries, or a KYB profile for corporate clients. A custom profile works everywhere a preset does.

## Apply one policy everywhere

A profile is the policy for every path a customer can take into your instance:

- **ID links:** a hosted verification link issued under a profile runs that profile's check, with its documents and its applicant type. The link keeps the classification it was issued with.
- **Customer records:** a customer created or updated under a profile is checked against its blocked countries.
- **On-chain verification:** a wallet registered on your identity register under a profile receives its investor type and is checked against its blocked countries.
- **Pending approvals:** an approved check waits until your wallet signs the registration, and the profile is applied again at that point.

Blocked countries are enforced off chain, on every path above, because no country is on chain to check. Adding a country to the list therefore does not change what a profile gate answers for a wallet that is already attested: revoke or freeze that wallet if it must stop. On an ERC-3643 eligibility register, adding a country queues the removal of every admitted wallet whose customer comes from that country, and nothing is written until your wallet signs.

## Take a verifier's KYC under a profile

A profile can also be served by a KYC source: a verifier you designate that already verified the investor, for example the liability umbrella at the time of investment. The source is tied to one profile and delivers each investor's KYC file as a [sealed KYC record](/protocol/infrastructure/sealed-kyc-records), encrypted to a key only your local tool holds. When you open a record, the wallet's customer record is created under the source's profile if none exists.

Trusset cannot read a sealed file, so it applies none of the profile's document or country rules to it. The review of the file is yours. An approved record admits the wallet to the whitelists bound to the source, with a transaction your wallet signs. It does not attest the wallet, so it neither makes the wallet eligible under the profile's gate nor admits it to an eligibility register.

## Change a profile safely

A profile in use carries real customers, so changes to it are controlled:

- **Reclassification needs confirmation.** Changing the classification, the professional-client rule or the applicant type of a profile in use must be confirmed before it applies. The change is written to the audit log with the customers it affects.
- **Disable instead of delete.** A disabled profile takes no new links, customers or wallets, while customers already under it keep working. A profile in use cannot be deleted, and its key cannot change.
- **Every admission keeps its requirements.** Each admission records a hash of the requirements it was made under. When the requirements change, admissions made under the earlier ones are flagged.

## Gate lending and vaults on a profile

A profile becomes a gate on chain. On your [instance ID register](/endpoints/customers/introduction#the-instance-id-register), you put the profile on the register in the Issuer Platform, under Customers, Configure, with a transaction your register admin wallet signs. The register creates the profile's own gate contract, and its address is what a lending market, a vault or a profile-restricted token points at.

The gate carries the rules that can be checked on chain:

| Rule on chain | Taken from the profile |
|---|---|
| Required predicates | `KYC_DONE`, plus `RESIDENCY_ON_FILE` when the profile requires a proof of address |
| Minimum investor class | Professional (`2`) when the profile is limited to professional clients |
| Maximum attestation age | The re-verification period |

A wallet is eligible as soon as an attester your register trusts vouches for it with those predicates and that class. Nothing is admitted or removed per wallet. Blocked countries, document requirements and the applicant type stay off chain: Trusset applies them before a wallet is attested, as described above.

Changes to a profile on chain are signed by your admin wallet as well. A disabled profile deactivates its gate, which then admits nobody. A profile on chain cannot be deleted. Some changes reach live markets and have to be acknowledged first: lifting the professional-client rule of a profile whose gate is the borrower gate of a market priced from a live market price, and disabling a profile whose gate is the borrower gate of any market. Such a market then takes no new loans, liquidity or vault funding until its admin chooses another gate. Repayments and collateral releases stay open.

A lending market priced from a live market price lends to professional borrowers only. Its borrower gate must be the gate of an active profile limited to professional clients. That gate refuses every wallet below the professional class on chain, and Trusset reads the gate back before installing it, refusing one that does not come from an instance ID register or has a lower minimum class. See [Professional borrowers](/endpoints/lending/set-identity-gates#professional-borrowers).

An instance that keeps its customers on its own ERC-3643 identity register gates with eligibility registers instead. An eligibility register holds exactly the wallets admitted under the profile. It is deployed for the profile in the Issuer Platform, or an existing register is bound to it, and admissions and removals are queued for your wallet to sign: a customer who passes an ID link review is queued for admission, and a revocation, a rejected review, a country added to the blocked list or an expired re-verification period queues a removal. Eligibility registers are available on Ethereum instances only. A profile limited to professional clients needs a register whose admissions your instance signs. See [Eligibility registers](/endpoints/customers/introduction#eligibility-registers).

## Set up a profile

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the profiles">
    In the [Issuer Platform](https://issuers.trusset.org/), open Customers, then Configure. The three presets are already there.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Define the policy">
    Adjust a preset, or add a custom investor type. Set its on-chain type, who may onboard, the professional-client rule and the re-verification period. Then choose the Sumsub level, the required documents and the blocked countries.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Onboard customers">
    Issue [ID links](/endpoints/customers/create-id-link) under the profile, or name its key when you verify a wallet through the API.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Gate markets and vaults">
    Put the profile on your instance ID register, then choose the profile as the gate of a lending market or vault. On an instance with its own ERC-3643 identity register, deploy or bind an eligibility register for the profile instead.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
Investor profiles are in beta. They are created and edited in the Issuer Platform. The API reads them with [List Verification Profiles](/endpoints/customers/list-verification-profiles), where they are called verification profiles.
</Note>
