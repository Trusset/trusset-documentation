A wallet can borrow or deposit only when it passes the gate the lender of record chose. Onboarding is getting a client's wallet through that gate. This page explains the gates once and maps every onboarding path onto them, for banks and fintechs alike.

## The gates

A market has two gates. The **borrower register** examines every loan and every further draw. The **provider register** is read by each vault that funds the market before it admits a depositor. A vault can also carry a register of its own, chosen at creation in the Issuer Portal. Without one, it adopts the provider register of the first market it funds and keeps it.

Every gate is one of these kinds:

| Gate | Admits | Who puts wallets on it |
|---|---|---|
| [Instance ID register](/endpoints/customers/introduction#the-instance-id-register) | Wallets that an attester the register trusts has attested on the identity hub with the register's required predicates, unless the register froze them | An attester the register trusts: the instance's own wallets, or another bank or KYC source the instance chose to rely on. Each attests under its own address |
| [Profile gate](/endpoints/customers/introduction#profile-gates) | Wallets the instance ID register verifies that also meet one [verification profile](/protocol/infrastructure/investor-profiles): its required predicates, minimum investor class and maximum attestation age | The same attesters. There is no admission per wallet |
| An instance's own register | Wallets verified on an ERC-3643 or Trusset-compatible register an instance bound as its own | That instance. Its wallet signs as agent of an ERC-3643 register, or with the roles the register's owner granted |
| Eligibility register | Exactly the wallets admitted under one verification profile of an instance that runs its own ERC-3643 register | The profile's instance. Admissions are queued for it and its wallet signs them |
| [Whitelist](/endpoints/lending/list-access-lists) | Exactly the wallets its owner and managers listed. It examines no identity | The owner and managers of the list |

Trusset holds no role on the identity hub, on an instance ID register or on a profile gate. A wallet attested by another institution passes your instance ID register only when your register trusts that institution's wallet as an attester. Relying on another party's KYC is your own decision, made on your register.

The market admin installs and changes the gates with [Set Identity Gates](/endpoints/lending/set-identity-gates). A change applies from the next loan, draw or deposit. Repaying, closing and redeeming are never gated.

Three rules limit the choice. A `MARKET`-priced market lends to professional clients only, so its borrower gate must be the profile gate or the eligibility register of a profile limited to professional clients, and never a whitelist. A profile gate counts only when it belongs to an instance ID register created by the register factory and its minimum investor class is 2 or higher. A whitelist becomes a provider gate only with the attestation that it lists identified wallets only. Eligibility registers run on Ethereum instances with their own ERC-3643 register. An instance on its instance ID register uses profile gates instead.

## The five onboarding paths

**1. Hosted ID link.** For a fintech without its own KYC process. [Create Customer](/endpoints/customers/create), then [Create ID Link](/endpoints/customers/create-id-link). The client opens the link on verify.trusset.org, signs in with the wallet and passes the identity check. The approval then waits in the [pending queue](/endpoints/customers/list-pending-verifications) until your wallet signs it onto your identity register. On an instance ID register your wallet can instead sign one batch, and each client submits their own claim from their wallet on verify.trusset.org: see [Holder claims](/endpoints/customers/introduction#holder-claims). To run the same check inside your own app, [Onboarding](/endpoints/customers/onboarding) takes one customer from the start to the signed registration through a single endpoint group, and follows a sealed record from your KYC source (path 4) the same way.

**2. Direct verification.** For an institution that ran KYC itself. [Verify Identity](/endpoints/customers/verify-identity) puts one wallet on your identity register with its `kycHash`, and [Batch Verify](/endpoints/customers/batch-verify) does many in one transaction. Your wallet signs. On an instance ID register, that is an attestation on the identity hub under your wallet's address.

**3. KYC proofs.** For an institution that holds the KYC data and wants claims backed by STARK proofs. [KYC-STARKs](/tools/kyc-starks/overview) runs on your machine and turns each record into a Merkle root, one proof per field and a header signed with your operator key. The root goes on chain as the `kycHash`. You verify the proofs on your side, and [Add Claims from Proof](/endpoints/customers/add-claims-from-proof) checks the signed header and adds the [predicates](/endpoints/customers/introduction#predicates) the leaves prove, such as residency on file. Trusset never receives the data or the proofs. A proof reveals the value it is about, so share one only with a party entitled to that value.

**4. Sealed KYC record.** For a bank that wants the KYC file itself from a verifier it trusts. The bank adds the verifier as a KYC source bound to its whitelists. The verifier seals the client's file with [KYC Courier](/tools/kyc-courier/overview) so that only the bank can open it, and delivers it. The bank opens it on its own machine and approves it, and its wallet lists the client's wallet on those whitelists. See [Sealed KYC Records](/protocol/infrastructure/sealed-kyc-records).

**5. Direct whitelist listing.** For the owner or a manager of a whitelist that already knows the wallets, such as a bank's existing clients. [Set Whitelist Members](/endpoints/lending/set-access-list-members) lists or removes up to 200 wallets per transaction. On a list bound to a KYC source, each wallet first needs an approved, valid sealed record.

On an instance ID register a profile gate reads each attestation directly, so nothing is queued. On an instance with its own ERC-3643 register, every registration through paths 1 to 3 also queues the wallet's admission to the eligibility register of the profile it was verified under, when that profile has one. [Request Admission](/endpoints/customers/request-admission) queues one by hand. The profile's instance signs the admission with [Build Admission](/endpoints/customers/build-admission). An approved sealed record admits a wallet to whitelists only, never to an eligibility register.

## Which path fits which gate

| The gate is | A wallet gets through by |
|---|---|
| Your instance ID register | Paths 1, 2 or 3, signed by a wallet your register trusts as an attester. A wallet another bank or KYC source attested passes when your register trusts that attester for the predicates it needs |
| A profile gate of your register | The same, when the attestation also meets the profile's rules |
| Another instance's ID register or profile gate | Paths 1, 2 or 3, run by an attester that register trusts. Your attestations count there only when that instance chose to trust your wallet |
| An instance's own register | Paths 1, 2 or 3, run by that instance |
| An eligibility register | An admission under its profile, queued by paths 1 to 3 and signed by the profile's instance |
| A whitelist | Path 4 or 5 |

## Check a wallet before it acts

[Get Identity Gates](/endpoints/lending/get-identity-gates) names the gates a market reads, and each vault record names its depositor gate. [Identity Status](/endpoints/customers/identity-status) shows a wallet on the identity register, and [Get Wallet Eligibility](/endpoints/customers/get-wallet-eligibility) on every profile gate or eligibility register of your instance.

A refusal names the gate that stopped the wallet. [Open Loan](/endpoints/lending/open-loan) answers `IDENTITY_NOT_VERIFIED` with the register, and the profile or whitelist behind it. A vault [Deposit](/endpoints/vaults/deposit) answers `DEPOSITOR_NOT_VERIFIED` the same way.

## Who does what

A **fintech** usually onboards its clients through paths 1 to 3, and acts as the verifier in path 4 when a bank asks for the file. A **bank** chooses the gates, receives and approves sealed records, keeps its whitelists and signs the admissions to its own profiles' registers. [For fintechs](/protocol/fintechs) and [For banks](/protocol/banks) put these steps in order.
